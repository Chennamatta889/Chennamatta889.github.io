How to Calculate Solar Payback Period

Understand simple solar payback in India and how bill savings, maintenance, grid tariffs and subsidies change the result.

A solar payback period is the time required for estimated net electricity savings to recover the money invested in a rooftop solar installation. It can be useful for comparing options, but it is not a guaranteed investment return.

The simplified formula

Simple payback (years) = net initial investment ÷ net annual savings.

In GharWatt's calculator, net initial investment is gross estimated installation price minus an illustrative central subsidy if eligible. Annual net savings are the annualized variable-electricity charge reduction minus assumed annual maintenance. The default maintenance assumption is 1% of installation price per year.

Small worked example

Suppose a system has a gross installed estimate of ₹1,80,000, potential central CFA of ₹78,000, monthly electricity savings of ₹2,400 and estimated annual maintenance of ₹1,800. Net initial outlay would be ₹1,02,000 if full CFA were received. Net annual saving in this simplified example would be ₹27,000, giving a simple payback of about 3.8 years.

This result ignores financing interest, insurance, panel degradation, eventual inverter replacement, electricity-price changes, fixed charges and the possibility of different export-credit rates. It should not be interpreted as a financial guarantee.

What can lengthen the payback?

  • Higher installation cost or less eligible subsidy.
  • Lower daily solar production due to shading and weather.
  • Lower valuable offset of grid purchases.
  • Higher recurring maintenance and component replacements.
  • Loan interest or other financing charges.
